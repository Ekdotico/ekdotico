## 2. Data model (migration `20261019000000_ownership_offers`)

- `OwnershipOffer` and `OwnershipOfferState` as Data models has them (`documentId`, `fromUserId`, `toUserId`, `state`, `createdAt`, `expiresAt`, `respondedAt`, `emailClaimedAt`), plus the departures of S17-8: relations from `fromUserId` and `toUserId` to `User` (`Restrict`), and `onDelete: Cascade` from the document (the permanent deletion of FR-ED-36 deletes the row). By hand: a partial unique index on `("documentId") WHERE "state" = 'PENDING'` (at most one pending offer, whatever two requests do at once) and a check that `fromUserId <> toUserId`.
- `Notification` as built in slice 13. The payload of an offer's notification is `{ "offerId": … }`, nothing else; the kind is `ownership_offer`.
- "Expired" is derived from `expiresAt`, as for invitations: a row stored `PENDING` past its expiry is never open (reads, the acceptance and the creation of a new offer all test the time), and is written `EXPIRED` by the hourly timer or by the next write that meets it. The notification of an expired offer is left out of the list from the moment of expiry and deleted by the timer.
- "Withdrawn without anyone's action" is written, not derived, in the transaction of each cause (section 3), and read again, defensively, wherever an offer is read: an offer is open only while its document is live, its recipient holds the Editor role on it **now**, and its maker is the document's Owner **now**.

## 3. Design

**The module** is `src/lib/documents/ownership.ts` (rules and transactions), with `ownership-texts.ts` (the words the server and the browser both show, importing nothing) and `ownership-answers.ts` (what the routes answer). Nothing in it is of Next.js.

**Every change holds the document's row** `FOR UPDATE` (`lockDocument`), as an invitation, its acceptance, slice 15 and slice 16 do, and **reads again under the lock** what authorised it: the caller's role and the permission set (`roleUnderLock`), the collaborator, the offer. So the one-Owner invariant holds under concurrency: two offers (the second finds one pending, and the partial index refuses it if the lock failed), two accepts (the second finds the offer accepted), an accept racing a withdrawal, an expiry, a removal, a leave, a change of role or a deletion (one after the other: whichever commits first decides, and the other reads what it left).

**Offering** (`makeOffer`): the Owner's role is read again under the lock (a request authorised before a transfer is refused by what the rows say now); the document is unrecorded and in a personal workspace (else 409 `not_transferable`); the collaborator named is **of this document** (a row of another document is no row: 404), holds `EDITOR` (else 409 `not_an_editor`) and has an address (a deleted account has none); offers past their time are written expired, and offers whose recipient no longer holds the role are written withdrawn; where one still stands, 409 `offer_pending`. Then the offer, with its notification, in the one transaction. After the response (`after`): the one email.

**Withdrawing** (`withdrawOffer`): the Owner's role read again under the lock; the pending offer, still open, becomes `WITHDRAWN` and its notification goes; 404 where there is none (an expired offer cannot be withdrawn: it is over).

**Answering** (`answerOffer`, and `viewOffer` for the page): the account is the session's, never the request's. An offer that does not exist, and an offer named for another account, answer the same neutral statement, "not open to this account", so that a stranger learns nothing (S13-3's rule); to the account it names, an offer that is not open answers "no longer open" (accepted by it and still held: the page leads to the document). Under the lock, in this order: the offer is pending and its time is ahead (the claim is one `UPDATE … WHERE state = 'PENDING' AND "expiresAt" > now AND "toUserId" = the account`, so that the time is the stored time, not the page's); the document is live, unrecorded, in a personal workspace and owned by the maker; the account holds `EDITOR` and has an address. Where one of the last conditions fails the offer is written `WITHDRAWN` and the answer is "no longer open". Declining: `DECLINED`, the notification gone. Accepting, in the same transaction:

1. the offer `ACCEPTED`;
2. the document's `ownerId` and `workspaceId` set to the account and its personal workspace (`updatedAt` is not named: a transfer is not an edit);
3. the account's `DocumentCollaborator` row deleted, and one written for the former Owner as `EDITOR` (`addedAt`, `roleSince` now);
4. the notification deleted (it stays only while the offer is pending);
5. `owner_changed` written by `securityLogIn`: a failed write rolls everything back.

After the commit, and never before: `collaborator-changed` for the former Owner and for the new one. A publish that fails prints one line and fails nothing (the re-check acts within 60 seconds, FR-ED-41).

**The causes of a withdrawal** call `withdrawOffersFor(tx, documentId, userId, now)` inside their own transaction, after the lock: a role change of the Editor named (`changeRole`, to any other role), a removal (`removeCollaborator`), a leave (`leaveDocument`). The deletion of a document (`deleteDocument`) calls `endOffersOf(tx, documentId, now)`, as it calls `endInvitationsOf`. `deleteDocument` also adds the Owner's identifier to the condition of its write, so that a deletion authorised just before a transfer commits does not delete the document in the name of someone who is no longer its Owner (it answers as for a document already deleted).

**The hourly timer** (`expireOffers`): in one transaction writes every pending offer past its time `EXPIRED` and deletes its notifications. Idempotent; with two instances the second finds nothing.

**The notification list** (additive): `listOfferNotifications` and `markOfferNotificationRead` are in `ownership.ts`. The route `GET /api/notifications` adds the offers to the one list and to the one unread count; `PATCH /api/notifications/[id]` marks an offer's notification where it is not an invitation's; the page and `NotificationList` show both kinds, newest first. Nothing of the invitation functions changes.

## 4. Routes, each with its authorisation

Every route that changes state checks the origin first, then reads the session's row (never the cookie cache), then, for a route with a document, the document and the permission (404 or 403, logged), then the body (415 where not JSON). `tests/unit/routes.test.ts` holds each handler under `[id]` to its permission; `route-origin` tests hold each handler to the origin check.
