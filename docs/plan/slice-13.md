## 2. Data model (migration `20261018000000_sharing`)

- `DocumentCollaborator` as Data models has it, with `onDelete: Cascade` from the document (the permanent deletion of FR-ED-36 deletes the row of a document) and `Restrict` from the user.
- `DocumentInvitation` as Data models has it, plus two departures, reported: `acceptedById` (the refusal of FR-ED-37 is for "an address at which a user who holds a role on the document accepted", which needs who accepted) and a relation from `invitedById` to `User` (`Restrict`). `onDelete: Cascade` from the document. By hand: a partial unique index on `(documentId, email) WHERE state = 'PENDING'` and a lower-case check on `email`, as for admission invitations.
- `Notification` as Data models has it. The payload of an invitation's notification is `{ "invitationId": … }`, nothing else.
- "Expired" is derived from `expiresAt`, as for admission invitations: a row still stored `PENDING` past its expiry is written `EXPIRED` by the next write that meets it (a new invitation to the address, a resend).

The uniform answer: the request writes the same rows for every address and returns. After the response (`after`), for each new invitation: look up a verified account with the address; skip where there is none, or where it is the Owner's or a collaborator's (FR-ED-44 (a)); otherwise create the notification, claim the row (`UPDATE … SET "emailClaimedAt" = now() WHERE "emailClaimedAt" IS NULL`), and hand over one email, once, with no retry (FR-ED-21: "where the outcome of a send is unknown the email is not sent a second time"). So neither the body nor the time of the response depends on whether an address has an account.

## 6. Security tests (each fails when its rule is broken; the weakening named)

1. Uniform answer: an address of a verified account, of an unverified account, and of none get byte-identical responses and identical rows; only the first gets an email and a notification (weakening: answer differently, or create no row for an unknown address).
2. No email or notification in the request: both happen only in the `after` task (weakening: send before returning).
3. At most once: running the hand-over task twice sends one email (weakening: drop the claim).
4. The token is stored only as a hash and never in a response, a notification, or a log line (weakening: return the link).
5. Acceptance needs the invited address, verified: another account, an unverified holder, and the link alone are refused, and nothing changes (weakening: accept by token alone).
6. Only pending: an accepted, declined, revoked, expired invitation grants nothing.
7. A holder of a role cannot accept an invitation created for it under FR-ED-44 (a).
8. Limit of ten: nine invitations pass, the tenth person is refused with 409 and nothing created; two concurrent requests cannot together pass ten (weakening: drop the lock on the document row).
9. Rate limit: the 21st invitation in an hour is 429 and creates nothing (weakening: count per request, or not at all).
10. Permission: a Viewer and an Editor without the permission get 403 (logged) on every invitation route; with the cell set, an Editor may invite, and only the Editor's own address is refused.
11. Origin first on every new state-changing route (the existing `route-origin` tests pick the new routes up).
12. Authorisation: every new route under `[id]` is made by `documentRoute` (the existing `routes` test).
13. A notification is visible to its recipient alone; PATCH by another account is a 404.
14. The acceptance writes `collaborators_changed` in its transaction; a failed log write leaves no collaborator.
15. A Viewer, admitted by the real `authorise` after acceptance, holds a read-only connection and what it sends is never applied or stored.
16. Deleting a document revokes its pending invitations and removes their notifications.

## 8. After the review of pull request 43

- An invitation is answered only where it was sent: its row was claimed for its email. One sent to no one (no account, or FR-ED-44 (a)) cannot be accepted or declined, by its link or from the list, even where its address later belongs to a verified account.
- An acceptance holds the document's row, as a creation does, so that a creation never counts one person fewer (ten people).
- The invitations of an hour are counted inside the transaction, after every refusal, and given back where the transaction then fails (S13-5).
- Marking a notification read answers 404 for one the list no longer shows. The people of a document carry no account identifier.
- Known and left: a person without a session who opens an invitation's link is sent to `/sign-in?return=/invitations/<token>`. The redirect and the invitation page send `no-referrer`; the sign-in page sends `strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, so the token reaches only this origin in a `Referer`, never another site. Sending `no-referrer` on the sign-in page for such a return path is a header rule matched on the query, which can be added if the owner wants it.
