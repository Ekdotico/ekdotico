## 3. Design

**The record is the gate, and it is read from the table.** `inDeletion(userId)` is one read by the unique index, never from a cache: the signed copy of a session is up to a minute old, and "the account can make no further request" must hold from the commit. It sits in `validate` of `session.ts`, which the proxy, every route, every page and the collaboration server all call, so there is one place to break it (S22-5). It answers no session, and so 401 (Q8), and hands on no renewed cookie.

**The race, closed in the database.** A creation of a document or an acceptance of an offer after the count would leave a deleted account the Owner of a document. The confirmation takes the account's row `FOR UPDATE`, those two take it `FOR SHARE` and ask for the record. Postgres makes one wait for the other; whichever is second reads the first. The order in which a transaction takes its rows is that of a change of the password (the credentials, the session, the account's row last), so that none waits for another in a circle.

**Steps.** `carryOutDeletions` finds the records not complete, takes each under a claim, and carries out the steps still owed in order. A step that fails is logged by its name and the class of the fault and stops that record for this run; the others go on. The claim is released at the end of the run, whatever happened, so that the next run (an hour on, or the timer at start) repeats the step; a claim an hour old is taken over, and every write of a step names the claim it was taken under, so a run that lost it writes nothing. (c) is the one step that is not a transaction of the database: the call is made, and the record of its outcome is the transaction.

**(e) and (f) are one transaction (S22-4).** FR-AU-58 lists (e) before (f), and (f) finds "every document invitation that was addressed to the user" by the address that (e) clears. In two transactions, (f) after (e) cannot find them. The data model has one column for both (`rowsDoneAt`).

**The tombstone keeps what the Docs allow** (section 2, `User`). The test reads every column of the row, and fails on one that is neither null nor in the allowed list.

## 5. Departures from Data models (to be carried to the page)

- `User.erasedAt` is on the page (a column of the model) and is built as it says.
- `AccountDeletion.feedbackSkippedAt` (S22-1): FR-AU-58 (c) says the step "is recorded as skipped", and the page says "`feedbackDoneAt` stays null". A null cannot tell a step that was skipped from one that is owed once the switch is on. (The plan's section 7, item 6, found this.)
- `AccountDeletion.claimedAt` (S22-2): the claim, in the manner of `Document.deletionClaimedAt` and `DocumentImage.deletionClaimedAt`.
- `AccountDeletion.userId` is a relation to `User` that restricts, so that the record cannot outlive its row or the reverse.
- Five checks in the migration hold the order of the steps (section 3).
- The page's `DocumentInvitation.titlePseudonym` row is still owed (D207) and now has its deletion rule.

## 6. Tests (each fails when its rule is broken)

Integration, one file at a time, `tests/integration/account-deletion.test.ts`, against PostgreSQL and Valkey, with the collaboration channel heard:

1. **Refusal.** The route answers 409 and states the count for an account that owns a live document, a deleted one within its retention, and one claimed for its permanent deletion; it writes no record; the password is not asked for first; nothing is counted as a failed sign-in. Broken by: counting only live documents; counting in the route and not under the lock.
2. **The race.** A creation of a document and a confirmation begun together: either the deletion is refused or the creation is (401); never both succeed. The same for the acceptance of an offer. Broken by: taking `FOR SHARE` out of `holdOwnableAccount`; the count before the lock.
3. **The prompt.** A wrong password: 403 with the statement of FR-AU-81, counted as a failed sign-in, nothing recorded; the twentieth locks the account (Q10). With two-factor authentication on: no code, a wrong code, a spent code, a recovery code: refused; a right code accepted. A password reset between the check and the record: refused (`outrun`); the session ended between: 401. Origin: 403 for another origin, nothing read. 415 for a body not sent as JSON; 422 for a body with another field. Broken by: skipping the check of the password; reading the session from the cookie copy; dropping `standing`.
4. **The gate (a).** After the record: every route answers the account 401 (the route table of `tests/routes.ts` is walked), a cookie copy that is still fresh included; a sign-in with the right password gets the statement of FR-AU-81 and counts toward nothing and extends nothing; the lock link, a second step begun before, and a reset request give nothing; a connection open before is closed within 5 seconds, by the event and, with the event dropped, by the re-check; the handshake is refused. Broken by: reading the table only when the cache is not used; removing the check from `checkCredentials`, from the second step, from the link.
5. **The steps.** In order, one entry each, with the record's times set; a failing step (b) stops (c) to (e) and is repeated by the next run, which resumes at that step; a crash after a step's work and before its record repeats the step without a second effect; the database refuses a time set out of order (each of the five checks, by a direct update). Two runs at once: one takes the claim, the other does nothing and says nothing; a claim an hour old is taken over, and the run that lost it writes nothing. Broken by: recording before the work; removing the claim's name from the write; removing a check of the migration.
6. **(b), against the port.** The no-op adapter: no call, the step done; an adapter that reports the capability and answers `pseudonymised`, `no-record`, `held`, an error, an outcome this version does not know, and an answer that leaves a document out: only the first two finish the step; the request identifier is the same on every attempt; only recorded documents are named.
7. **(c).** Switch off (unset, empty, `false`): the intake is not called in any run, the step is recorded skipped with its entry, the deletion completes. On (`true`): called once, acknowledged: done with its entry; not acknowledged, rejected, resolved false: not recorded, repeated, the deletion held at (c) and not completed. A value that is neither: fails and says so. The default client throws. Broken by: reading the switch inverted; calling before reading it; recording a skip as done.
8. **(d).** Sessions (several), the credential, the second factor, every kind of verification row of the account (reset, second step, lock link, a pending change holding a new address), and a row keyed by the address: all gone; another account's rows all stay. A sign-in that began before is found to have no credential and leaves no session.
9. **(e) and (f), the tombstone.** Every column of the row is checked: null, or in the allowed list (`id`, `erasedAt`, the acknowledgement and its version, the table's own `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, and the flags at their empty values). The address is free again (an admission to it makes an account). Admission invitation: the address cleared on the one the account was made from and on an earlier one for the same address; the row stays; the list shows it without one. Document invitations: pending ones sent and received revoked with their notifications; the address cleared on those addressed to it, accepted ones included; the pseudonym of every invitation it sent stripped of the name, the two words kept; invitations of others unchanged. Offers: pending offers to it declined, the Owner told it ended and shown "the Editor"; offers past their time left to the timer. Roles: collaborator rows deleted on live and deleted documents, the others' unchanged; the content it contributed in a document still there. Notifications of the account kept, and the list of the tombstone is empty because it has no session. A repetition changes nothing.
10. **Surfaces.** The sender of an accepted invitation reads "Deleted account" in the page and in the notification; the pseudonym reads "Flying Hippos — Deleted account"; the notice of an ended offer reads "the Editor".
11. **NFR-DP-06.** A deletion whose step failed for two runs completes at the third, and its `completedAt` is within the month of its `requestedAt`; a deletion open past a month is said in the run's line; a refusal leaves no record, so no month runs.
