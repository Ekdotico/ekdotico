## 2. Data model

One migration, `20261027000000_ownership_offer_batch`: `OwnershipOffer.batchId uuid NULL`. The identifier of the transfer-of-all action that made the offer; null for an offer made one document at a time. It groups the one email to a person (FR-AU-92: "Offers made to one person in one action are announced by one email") and lets the deletion screen list the documents of this action that were accepted. No new table. Personal data: none (an identifier, no field about a person); it goes with the offer. Data models carries a note (section 5).

## 3. Design

**One transaction.** In order, and the order is the order of slices 22 and 23 (document before account): (1) the session was read by the route; (2) every document the account owns, in every state, locked `FOR UPDATE` in the order of the identifiers, by a statement of its own that does not filter on `deletedAt` (a deleted document is read, not offered); (3) the pending offers of those documents settled (`settleOffers`, the backstop of slice 17: an expired offer is written expired, an offer whose Editor has lost the role is written withdrawn); (4) the Editors of the documents read, and the accounts of the possible recipients locked `FOR SHARE` in the order of their identifiers, then asked for their deletion; those that are gone or in deletion are left out; (5) the plan made (`planTransfer`), the identifiers compared as sets with the request's (409 `changed`), the person's row resolved under the locks (404), and the count of new offers taken (`OFFERS_PER_USER`, 409 above the limit's whole figure, 429 otherwise); (6) one offer and one notification for each new offer, in the same transaction.

**Why every owned document is locked, and the recipients' accounts held.** A change of role, a removal, an invitation accepted, a deletion, a restore, an offer and its acceptance all take the document's row; held, they are one after the other and the loser reads what the winner left. The recipient's account row held `FOR SHARE` makes the confirmation of the recipient's deletion (`FOR UPDATE`) wait for the offer, or the offer find the confirmation: either the offer is made first and the step that declines the pending offers of a deleted account (`declineOffersTo`) ends it, or it is not made. Two bulk actions at once, or one with a single offer, wait for each other on the first locked row and then see the pending offers and keep them.

**One plan, in one place.** `planTransfer(documents, choice, now)` is pure: for each document it answers `offer` (to whom), `keep` (a pending offer) or `skip` (with the reason). The screen counts with it from an unlocked read; the transaction decides with it from the locked one. The two cannot differ in the rule.

**The screen.** The deletion screen of an account that owns documents gains a section, "Transfer ownership of all documents": the document list with the state of each, and the control, which opens a dialog (focus on Cancel, as for a clearing) with the choice, the confirmation's numbers and the control. The choice is preselected on "To the longest-standing Editor", so that the action and its confirmation are two clicks (FR-AU-58). From `@ekdotico/domico` 0.41.0 only: `RadioGroup`, `Select`, `Dialog`, `Button`, `FormFault`, `Stack`, `Cluster`. Nothing is missing.

**Email.** After the response (`afterResponse`), one `deliverBatch` for each recipient. It claims the person's unclaimed open offers of the batch in one `UPDATE ... RETURNING`, and sends one email whose number is the number it claimed. An account in deletion is sent nothing.

## 5. Departures from Data models (to be carried to the page)

- `OwnershipOffer.batchId` (S24-5). The page lists the model's fields; this one is not there.
- `GET /api/account/deletion`: the page says it answers "for each document the state of its offer of ownership, if any". It answers the documents the account owns, and the accepted documents of this action (S24-6), each with its state. It sends titles of the account's own documents and the display name of the Editor an offer is made to, and no address and no identifier of another account.
- API design, `POST /api/account/documents/transfer`: "The answer lists the documents that could not be offered because they have no Editor". The answer is the numbers (offered, kept, cannot be offered); the list is on the page it is read from, with the reason for each (the states of FR-AU-92), because a list with a reason for each is longer than "no Editor".

## 6. Tests (each fails when its rule is broken)

Unit, `tests/unit/transfer-plan.test.ts` (the plan is pure): the longest-standing Editor by `roleSince`; a tie by `addedAt`, then by identifier; a former Owner counts from the time of the transfer; an Editor passed over after a decline or an expiry, and not after a withdrawal; a pending offer is kept for any choice; a deleted document, one past its day, a document in a notice period (offered), a document that is not transferable, and a document with no Editor are skipped with their reasons; "To one person" offers only the documents on which the person is an Editor; an account in deletion is never chosen.

Integration, `tests/integration/transfer.test.ts` and `tests/integration/transfer-races.test.ts`, one file at a time against PostgreSQL and Valkey:

1. **Authorisation.** No session: 401. Another origin: 403 before the body is read. A session whose row has ended: 401. The body names only the account's own documents: a document of another account in `documentIds` is a 409 and no offer is made; a `collaboratorId` of a document of another account is a 404; a Viewer's or Reviewer's row is a 404, not an offer. Broken by: the lock reading every account's documents; the person's row not tied to the caller's documents; the role not asked.
2. **Nothing left out, nothing added.** The identifiers must be exactly the documents offered or kept now: one more, one fewer, one not owned, one deleted since, one that changed hands since: 409, no offer, no notification, no email. A document that gained its first Editor after the screen is read: 409. Broken by: the comparison out of the transaction; comparing counts.
3. **The rule (a) and (b) end to end.** The longest-standing Editor is offered each document (several Editors, a tie, a former Owner); "To one person" offers only the documents they edit; the others are counted as not offered. Each offer is a row of FR-ED-47: pending, fourteen days, a notification for the Editor with the kind and payload of slice 17, the same expiry for all.
4. **Per-document states.** Every state of the screen, each by a document: offered (to whom), declined, expired, without an Editor, not offered yet, deleted, being deleted, accepted. A document with a pending offer is counted as offered and keeps its Editor and its row (no second offer, no second notification). After a decline the document is offered to the next Editor, not the same one; after an expiry as well.
5. **One Owner.** After any mix of accepts and declines of the offers of one action, every document has exactly one Owner who is a user with a personal workspace, and the former Owner is an Editor of those accepted; no document is left with none or two. (The acceptance is slice 17's; this proves the bulk leaves it intact.)
6. **Exclusions.** A deleted document within its retention is not offered; a document whose clearing day has come is not (and is "being deleted"); a document in its notice period is offered, and the acceptance takes it out of the clearing (the slice-23 path, through a bulk offer); a recorded document and one in an institutional workspace are not transferable. Broken by: each of those conditions removed.
7. **The account in deletion.** The caller whose deletion is confirmed makes nothing (401 and a direct call of the library: no offer); a recipient whose deletion is confirmed is never chosen and never picked, and the next Editor is; a recipient who is a tombstone (no address) is never chosen. Broken by: `inDeletion` out of the plan; the accounts not locked.
8. **Races.** Two actions at once make one set of offers and the second keeps the first's. An action and a single offer: one offer for the document. An action and the confirmation of the recipient's deletion: either the offer is declined by the step of the deletion or none is made, never an offer pending to a tombstone; an action and the caller's own clearing confirmation: either the clearing records the document and the offer is made or refused with the document read in its state, never a document cleared with an offer that outlives it; an action and the removal of an Editor, a change of role, the deletion of a document, its restore: the plan reads the winner's state. A lock order check: the action, a clearing, the deletion's confirmation and an acceptance begun together finish (no deadlock). Broken by: the locks out; the order of the account row and the documents reversed.
9. **The limit.** Each new offer is counted; a kept one is not; an action over the allowance: 429 with the time, nothing offered, nothing counted; over the limit's whole figure: 409 stating it; a failure after the count gives it back. Broken by: counting one for the action; counting kept offers.
10. **Emails.** One email to each person, with the number of documents of that person; the offer to one person who is offered one document reads FR-ED-47's text; no title or content in any email or log line (the titles are a marker string searched in every sink and log line); claimed once on two concurrent deliveries and by the timer; an offer that ended before the delivery is not counted; none to an account in deletion. The sink is the local sink; no real email.
11. **The screen's library and `GET /api/account/deletion`.** The same states by the route; no address and no identifier of another account in the answer; another account's documents are not listed.
12. **The timer.** `ownership-offer-email` hands over the batch offers that a stopped process left unclaimed, once; the single-offer path never sends an email for a batch offer.
